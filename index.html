Understanding Mental Health

Common Conditions — Explained with Care

Knowledge is the first step toward healing. Learn the signs, understand what you or a loved one might be experiencing, and know that help is available.

Most Common

Depression

Depression is more than sadness — it's a persistent shadow that can affect how you feel, think, and handle daily activities. It is a real medical condition that requires real care and compassion.

Persistent sadness, emptiness, or hopelessness lasting weeks
Loss of interest in things you once enjoyed
Fatigue, low energy, or feeling slowed down
Changes in appetite — eating too much or too little
Difficulty concentrating, remembering, or making decisions
Thoughts of death or suicide — please reach out immediately
Get Support Now →
Very Common

Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety goes beyond everyday worry. It can be overwhelming, disabling, and very real.

Excessive worry that is hard to control
Restlessness, feeling on edge or wound up
Panic attacks — sudden intense fear with physical symptoms
Avoidance of situations that trigger worry
Learn More →
Trauma-Related

PTSD

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder can develop after witnessing or experiencing a deeply distressing event.

Flashbacks or recurring nightmares about the trauma
Feeling emotionally numb or detached
Being easily startled or feeling constantly on guard
Avoiding reminders of the traumatic event
Find Help →
Mood Disorder

Bipolar Disorder

Bipolar disorder causes extreme mood swings between emotional highs (mania) and deep lows (depression).

Periods of extreme energy, euphoria, and reduced need for sleep
Followed by deep depressive episodes
Impulsive behavior during manic phases
Difficulty maintaining relationships or work
Learn More →
Psychotic

Schizophrenia

A serious but manageable condition affecting how a person thinks, feels, and behaves.

Hallucinations — hearing or seeing things others don't
Delusions — strongly held false beliefs
Disorganized thinking or speech
Withdrawal from family and friends

Mental Health Awareness

Breaking the Silence — One Conversation at a Time

Mental health stigma is a barrier to healing. By educating ourselves and our communities, we save lives. Here's how awareness works:

01

Recognize the Signs

Learn to identify behavioral changes — withdrawal, mood swings, sleep disruption — in yourself and loved ones before they escalate.

02

Start the Conversation

A simple "Are you okay?" can be life-saving. Listening without judgment is one of the most powerful acts of compassion.

03

Seek Professional Help

Mental health conditions are medical conditions. Encourage treatment — therapy, medication, or crisis support — without shame.

04

Build a Support System

Community, family, and friends play a vital role in recovery. Being present matters more than having the perfect words.

🎗️ Suicide Prevention

Every Life is Irreplaceable

Suicide is preventable. If you or someone you know shows warning signs — talking about death, giving away belongings, withdrawing from loved ones, or expressing hopelessness — please act immediately. You could save a life by simply staying present and listening.

⚠️
Warning Signs
  • • Talking about wanting to die
  • • Feeling like a burden
  • • Extreme mood swings
  • • Giving away possessions
🤲
How to Help
  • • Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?"
  • • Listen without judgment
  • • Stay with them
  • • Call crisis line together
💚
Recovery is Real
  • • Suicidal crises are temporary
  • • Treatment works
  • • You are not alone
  • • Hope exists always
